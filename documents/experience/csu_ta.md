# Teaching Assistant — Algorithms & Paradigms

**Dates:** August 2025 – December 2025
**Organization:** CSU Sacramento
**Course:** Algorithms & Paradigms (combined undergraduate and graduate)

## Role summary

Octavio was directly recruited by the professor to serve as Teaching Assistant for Algorithms & Paradigms after performing well as a student in the same course. The course covered a broad set of algorithmic topics across both undergraduate and graduate students. Octavio's responsibilities centered on grading, student support, and feedback coordination with the professor.

## What Octavio did

- Graded 5 multi-week assignments across 44 students, leaving detailed written feedback on each submission to help students improve their problem-solving approach and algorithm analysis.
- Held weekly virtual office hours on Discord (1+ hour per session at a consistent schedule) to answer student questions and reinforce course concepts.
- Answered student questions asynchronously throughout the semester via Discord.
- Identified recurring patterns and common mistakes across student submissions and surfaced them to the professor so he could address high-frequency misunderstandings in upcoming lectures if needed.
- Coordinated with the professor throughout the semester to confirm grading standards, align on feedback expectations, and receive instructions for upcoming assignments.

## Course topics covered

- Time and space complexity analysis — deriving and calculating complexity bounds using geometric and arithmetic series, and applying the Master Theorem as a more direct method for divide-and-conquer recurrences
- Dynamic programming
- Backtracking
- Greedy algorithms
- Sorting algorithms
- Graph algorithms — Dijkstra's shortest path, topological sort, and related techniques
- NP-completeness — conceptual coverage

## Why this role matters

Being recruited directly by a professor to grade a combined undergrad/grad algorithms course is a signal that Octavio's understanding of the material was recognized at a level above the typical student. The role required not just knowing the algorithms but being able to evaluate others' reasoning about them and communicate clearly where that reasoning broke down.
